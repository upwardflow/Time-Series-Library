\title{TimeRole: Role-differentiated historical modeling for multivariate long-term time-series forecasting}

\author{Yanlei Wang}
\author{Jin Zhang}
\author{Yiqun Lu}
\author{Junfeng Lei\corref{cor1}}
\ead{Jflei@whu.edu.cn}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}
\affiliation{organization={School of Electronic Information, Wuhan University},
             city={Wuhan},
             postcode={430072},
             country={China}}

\begin{abstract}
Multivariate long-term time-series forecasting requires models to capture current dynamics while exploiting information distributed across extended histories. Existing approaches often process all observations with a unified backbone and at a common resolution, although recent and distant observations may play different predictive roles. This uniform treatment can blur the distinction between current-state modeling and long-range contextual support, while increasing computation as the input window grows. To address this issue, we propose TimeRole, a history-role-differentiated forecasting framework that partitions the input into a recent window and distant history according to their temporal distance from the forecast origin. Statistics derived from the recent window normalize both segments within a common reference frame. For recent observations, a recent graph-enhanced state-space predictor (RGSP) combines series decomposition, dual-scale patch representations, bidirectional Mamba encoding, and variable-graph propagation to construct a base forecast. For distant observations, a distant-history corrector (DHC) compresses earlier history into low-resolution memory tokens and derives a conditional correction relative to the recent-state forecast. The base forecast and conditional correction are combined in the forecast space and optimized under a unified objective. By assigning recent and distant observations complementary modeling responsibilities, TimeRole decouples accessible historical coverage from the sequence length processed by the computationally intensive forecasting backbone, providing a structured framework for long-context multivariate forecasting.
\end{abstract}
